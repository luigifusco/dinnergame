# Rising Stakes

**The dough rises. So do the stakes.**

**Status: concept draft.** Nothing here is printed or implemented yet, and
[Sideplot](../sideplot/README.md) is unaffected. The
[first-game decisions](#decisions-for-the-first-game) are recorded below.
"Rising Stakes" is a working title; *Proof of Stake* and *Bread & Circuses* are
alternatives.

## Pitch

Everyone bakes their own focaccia, but every topping must be won, gambled for,
bought, or traded. Each ingredient card is a voucher for a real portion in the
pantry. When the dough has finished rising, the market closes and you top your
focaccia with whatever you're holding.

The dough's final rise is the game. The bake is the final round. Dinner is the
reveal.

## Design pillars

1. **A card is a portion.** You can only use what you hold at assembly; two
   copies make a double portion.
2. **Nobody eats badly.** Dough, olive oil, flaky salt, and brine are free. A
   plain Genovese is still a good dinner, and it can still win.
3. **Toppings are never forced.** Cards can be lost, stolen, or traded, but
   nothing goes on a focaccia without its owner's consent.
4. **The dough is the clock.** The host closes the market when the dough is
   ready, not when an arbitrary timer rings.
5. **Many ways in.** Skill, luck, bluffing, and dealing all pay. A player who
   loses every minigame can still trade their way to a great focaccia.
6. **Real taste drives trading.** The vegetarian holding prosciutto, the
   anchovy hater, and the player planning a sweet focaccia value the same card
   differently. That's the market.

## Components

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Ingredient cards | Printed vouchers for one portion each, in three tiers. |
| Dried beans | Currency for bets, bids, and the shop: about 15 per player plus a bank. |
| The pantry | Labeled bowls holding exactly one prepared portion per card in play. |
| Personal pans | One per player, such as a 20 cm round tin, with a name label. |
| Props | Two dice, a tombola set, small cups, a jar of dried pasta, a kitchen scale. |
| Golden rolling pin | The trophy: any rolling pin and gold spray paint. |

Beans are a nod to the Italian family habit of playing tombola and cards for
dried beans or lentils. They're cheap, tactile, and obviously not toppings.

## Ingredient cards

Cards are poker size, 63 x 88 mm. The front shows only a linocut-style
illustration of the portion and the ingredient's name, as in the
[mock-up](mockups/cherry-tomatoes.png). The OVEN or FINISH label and the portion
are on the pantry bowls and in the [ingredient list](INGREDIENTS.md), printed as
a chart for the table.

- **Tier** is shown by the card back, a tablecloth of wavy, translucent bands
  that darken where they cross: red for **Common**, green for **Fine**, and
  blue for **Luxury**. Backs are visible, so everyone can see who is hoarding
  Luxury cards, but not which ones.
- **OVEN** cards go on before baking. **FINISH** cards go on afterwards, like
  burrata, mortadella, arugula, and honey.
- **Capacity:** at most **6 OVEN** and **3 FINISH** cards per focaccia. Extra
  cards are for trading.

### The deck

The [ingredient list](INGREDIENTS.md) is an autumn deck of 80 cards for eight
players: four copies of each Common, two of each Fine, and one of each Luxury.
It gives every card's portion, a shopping list, and allergen notes.

## The evening

| Phase | Length | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Il Battesimo** | 10 min | Explain the basics. Everyone stretches a dough ball into their own pan and names it. Deal 3 random Commons and 10 beans to each player. |
| **The Market** | ~90 min | Hosted events, side bets, and private trading over aperitivo, while the pans rise where everyone can see them. |
| **Last Call** | 10 min | The host warns that the dough is nearly ready. The jar is revealed; final deals. |
| **Assembly** | 15 min | Spend OVEN cards at the pantry. Dimple, top, brine, bake. |
| **The Bake** | 20-25 min | The final round, for FINISH cards only. |
| **The Feast** | Rest of the night | Finish, taste everything, vote. |

The host decides when the dough is ready with the poke test, so nobody knows
exactly when the market will close.

## Earning cards

The bank keeps one face-down pile per tier. Events pay from those piles or in
beans; if a pile runs out, pay from the tier below. Run four to six hosted
events during the market, alternating kinds, and explain each one when it starts
rather than front-loading the rules.

### Hosted events

| Event | Kind | What players do | Prizes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Naso d'Oro** | Senses | Smell six covered cups of herbs and spices and write down guesses. | 1 bean per correct answer; the best score draws a Fine. |
| **Il Forno** | Push your luck | Roll for a bake total; see below. | Common, Fine, or Luxury. |
| **Mimo** | Social | Act out a card from the Common pile without words. | The first correct guesser keeps the card; the actor gets 2 beans. |
| **Il Pugno** | Skill | Scoop exactly 100 g of flour by eye, then weigh it. | Closest draws a Fine, second closest a Common. |
| **Tre Carte** | Gambling | Stake 3 beans and follow a Fine card through the host's three-card shuffle. | Find it to keep it and your stake; miss and the bank keeps the stake. |
| **Mise en Place** | Race | Strip rosemary, peel garlic, or pluck grapes for the pantry. No knives. | The fastest draws a Fine; every helper gets 2 beans. |
| **Tombola** | Luck | Italian bingo. Everyone gets one ticket; extras cost 2 beans. | Ambo and terno: Common. Quaterna and cinquina: Fine. Tombola: Luxury, plus every bean paid for extra tickets. |
| **L'Asta** | Economy | Open auction of three or four lots, paid in beans to the bank. | For example, one Luxury, a mortadella and pistachio bundle, and one face-down lot. |

A recommended first program: Naso d'Oro, Il Forno, Mimo, L'Asta, then Tombola
as the market winds down. More ideas: food trivia (*Vero o Falso*), pick-up
sticks with breadsticks (*Grissini Shanghai*), and tossing corks into an empty
pan.

### Il Forno

Roll two dice and add them to your bake total. If either die shows a 1, the
focaccia burns and your turn ends with nothing. Stop whenever you like and
collect one prize for the highest threshold you've reached.

| Total | Prize | Chance of reaching it if you keep rolling |
|---|---|---|
| 12+ | Common | 48% |
| 25+ | Fine | 28% |
| 40+ | Luxury | 14% |

Standing on a Fine at 25, pushing on for Luxury is close to a coin flip (44%).

### Always on

- **Morra duels.** Any two players can play Morra for stakes they both put on
  the table. Each shows zero to five fingers while shouting a guess at the
  total; a correct guess wins a point, and the first to three points takes the
  stakes. You can always decline.
- **The jar.** Guess how many pieces of pasta are in a jar before Last Call. The
  closest guess draws a Fine; an exact guess draws a Luxury.
- **Kitchen bounties.** The host pays beans for help: refilling glasses,
  clearing plates, washing a bowl.
- **The Bottega.** A self-service bank: put beans in the bowl and draw a random
  Common for 3 or a random Fine for 6. Sell any card back for 1 bean. Luxury is
  never for sale, and the Bottega closes when a pile runs out.

## Trading

- Trade anything, any time, with anyone: cards, beans, favors, and promised
  slices of your future focaccia.
- Trade privately. Whisper, take it to the kitchen, or step onto the balcony.
  You may lie about what you hold.
- A hand-to-hand swap is final. Spoken promises aren't enforced.
- **Napkin contracts** are. A deal written on a napkin and signed by both
  players is binding, and the host enforces it, including slices owed at dinner.
- OVEN cards stop mattering when your pan goes in. FINISH cards, beans, and
  promises keep trading until the feast.

## Assembly and the bake

1. The market closes for OVEN cards when the host declares the dough ready.
2. Hand up to 6 OVEN cards to the pantry keeper and take exactly those portions.
   Olive oil, flaky salt, brine, and black pepper are free.
3. Dimple, top, and bake at 220-230 °C for about 20-25 minutes. If the oven
   can't take every pan, bake in two waves in the order players finish
   assembling.

Before dealing, the host sets aside a **bake reserve** of FINISH cards, which
pays out while the focaccias are in the oven:

- **I Pacchi.** Wrap one napkin per player: one holds a Luxury, one is empty,
  and the rest hold Common or Fine cards. Each player may buy one for 3 beans.
  Before it's opened, the host, as *il Dottore*, offers a face-up card from the
  bank instead. Accept, or open?
- **L'Ultima Asta.** Auction the remaining reserved Luxury cards. Beans are
  worthless after this, so this is where everyone goes all in.

## The feast

1. Take out the focaccias and spend up to 3 FINISH cards at the pantry.
2. Each player announces their focaccia's name. Cut each one in half: one half
   goes on a shared tasting board, and the other half is yours.
3. Vote privately with the same ballot as the Sideplot quirk podium: **2
   points** for your favorite and **1 point** for your second favorite, never
   your own. Ask one question: *Which focaccia would you most like to eat
   again?*
4. Ties share a place. The winner takes the golden rolling pin and keeps it
   until the next dinner.

Optionally, the table can award *Il Disastro* to the most chaotic focaccia by
acclamation. The prize is a burnt breadstick.

## Optional modules

Not part of the first game. Add one at a time once the core has been played.

### Imprevisti

One-use chaos cards, named after the Chance pile in Italian Monopoly. Draw one
when your focaccia burns in Il Forno, or buy one at the Bottega for 3 beans.

| Card | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Arriva la Finanza** | Audit a player: they show you their hand and pay you one card of their choice. |
| **Malocchio** | When another player wins a prize, take it instead. |
| **Cornetto** | Cancel an Imprevisto played against you. |
| **Il Pacco** | Swap one of your cards, face down, for a random card from another player's hand. |
| **Ho un Cugino** | Exchange one of your cards for any card of the same tier in the bank. |
| **Seconda Lievitazione** | Ignore one burnt roll in Il Forno, or have a second attempt at a skill event. |
| **Sciopero** | Choose a player. They can't trade until the next event ends. |
| **Condono** | After losing a bet, take your stake back. |

### Secret orders

Deal two orders per player; each keeps one secret. At assembly, show it: if your
OVEN cards fulfill it, take 5 beans for the bake round. Orders give players a
different reason to want the same cards, and a reason to bluff.

| Order | Requirement for your OVEN cards |
|---|---|
| **Barese** | Cherry tomatoes, olives, and oregano. |
| **Tricolore** | Something red, something white, and something green. |
| **Il Minimalista** | Exactly one card. Sell everything else. |
| **Doppio Doppio** | Three copies of the same ingredient. |
| **Autunno** | Any two of pumpkin, pear, mushrooms, sausage, and gorgonzola. |
| **La Faccia** | Toppings arranged as a face. The table decides. |

### Sideplot crossover

Deal Sideplot hands as well. Show a completed mission privately to the host to
draw a Common, then draw a replacement mission as usual. Hold the Sideplot
reveal and quirk podium during the bake instead of I Pacchi; first, second, and
third place earn 6, 4, and 2 beans for the last auction.

## Host notes

- **Dough.** Cold-ferment individual dough balls of about 250 g the day before.
  Take them out about an hour before guests arrive; the rise in the pan is the
  market. Do a test run, because kitchen temperature changes everything.
- **Oven.** Count how many pans fit at once, and preheat during the last event.
- **Pantry.** Prepare one bowl per ingredient with exactly one portion per card
  in the deck, labeled with OVEN or FINISH and the portion; the
  [shopping list](INGREDIENTS.md#shopping-list) covers the whole deck. Keep
  FINISH ingredients in the fridge. Leftover portions become antipasti or
  tomorrow's lunch.
- **Diets.** Ask about diets and allergies beforehand. Meat and fish can stay,
  since vegetarians can trade them away. Leave out anything someone is seriously
  allergic to: the tasting board makes cross-contact hard to avoid. The
  [ingredient list](INGREDIENTS.md#diets-and-allergens) flags nuts, fish, pork,
  and milk.
- **Roles.** The host plays. A co-host runs the bank, the pantry, the
  auctions, and Tre Carte. If the co-host also plays, the host takes over
  whenever the co-host is bidding or betting.
- **Group size.** The [deck](INGREDIENTS.md) suits eight; scale it at about ten
  cards per player. With four or five players, run three events. With more than
  ten, play in pairs sharing a pan and a hand.

## Printing later

The [print test](mockups/cherry-tomatoes.pdf) is one duplex A4 sheet of nine
cherry tomatoes, previewed in [sheet.png](mockups/sheet.png). Its layout is the
guideline for the generator:

- **Cards touch.** The nine cards sit edge to edge in a 3 x 3 grid of
  189 x 264 mm, with no gaps, so one cut separates two neighbors. The front
  background runs 1.5 mm past the outside of the grid.
- **The back is one continuous tablecloth** covering the grid and 6 mm beyond,
  so duplex drift never shows and nothing needs to line up. The pattern repeats
  exactly three times across a card and four times down (21 x 22 mm), so every
  back is the same, with a crossing at its center.
- **Cut guides** are short lines in the front's margin, outside the cards, at
  both ends of each of the eight cut lines. Cut straight from guide to guide.
  The back has no guides, since it doesn't line up with the front.
- **One tier per sheet**, since a sheet has a single cloth color. The 80-card
  deck takes ten sheets: six Common, three Fine, and one Luxury, with ten spare
  slots.
- **Spread each ingredient's copies over different sheets.** Each sheet's cloth
  lands at its own small offset, so a sheet of one ingredient could be spotted
  from behind.

[Sideplot's generator](../sideplot/README.md#generate-the-pdfs) could carry the
deck with its duplex backs and the `copies` field for duplicates. It would need:

- The layout above. Sideplot's cards are 88 x 31.5 mm, 16 per sheet, each with
  its own crop marks and a back inset 2 mm from the cut.
- New card types with an illustration and a name, and the three tablecloth
  backs, plus Imprevisti and orders if used. The generator currently accepts
  only quirks and missions (`CardKind` in `sideplot\scripts\generate_cards.py`).
- One illustration per ingredient in `rising-stakes\art\`, in the style of
  [the cherry tomatoes](art/cherry-tomatoes.png).
- A card library in `rising-stakes\cards\`, printed with the existing `--cards`
  option. Once both games print cards, the generator could move out of
  `sideplot\` into shared tooling.
- Optionally, structured portion quantities, so the generator could also print
  the shopping list and the ingredient chart.

## Decisions for the first game

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Group size | Eight players, using the [autumn deck](INGREDIENTS.md). |
| Currency | Dried beans. |
| Modules | None. The core already has gambling and trading; keep the modules for later games. |
| Host role | The host plays. A co-host runs the bank, the pantry, the auctions, and Tre Carte. |
| Cards | Poker size, printed edge to edge with one continuous back per sheet. The front has only the illustration and the name in a playful hand-drawn font; the back is a wavy tablecloth, red, green, or blue by tier. |

Still open: how many pans fit in the oven at once.

## What a first playtest should tell us

- Whether everyone ended with enough cards for a focaccia they were happy with.
- Whether private trading happened without prompting, or needed a nudge.
- Which events dragged, and whether beans were saved, spent, or ignored.
- How long the dough actually took, and whether the market closing felt
  dramatic or rushed.
