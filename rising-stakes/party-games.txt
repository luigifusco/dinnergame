WORDS AND GUESSING
Taboo: describe a word to your team without saying any of the five forbidden words on its card.
Heads Up: hold a word to your forehead while your team shouts clues; get as many as you can in a minute.
Who Am I?: a name is stuck to your forehead; ask yes-or-no questions until you guess it.
Password: partners take turns giving one-word clues, and every failed clue lowers the points.
Just One: everyone writes a one-word clue for the guesser, and identical clues cancel out before they see them.
Contact: the keeper thinks of a word and reveals its first letter; the others give clues for words with that start and try to "contact" before the keeper guesses.
Wavelength: a clue-giver sees a hidden target on a scale like "boring to wild topping" and gives a clue; the team guesses where it sits.
Song Association: the host calls a word, and you have five seconds to sing a lyric that contains it.
Hum It: hum a song with your mouth closed; the first to name it wins.
Scattergories: roll a letter, then write an answer starting with it for each of ten categories; only answers nobody else wrote score.
Categories: in a clapping rhythm, everyone names something in a category, like cheeses; hesitate or repeat and you're out.
Fictionary: read out an obscure dish; everyone writes a fake description, then guesses the real one, scoring for every player they fool.
Herd Mentality: everyone writes an answer to a question like "best pizza topping"; only the answers that match the majority score.
Wits and Wagers: everyone guesses a number, like how many seeds are in a pomegranate, then bets on whose guess is closest without going over.

DRAWING AND MAKING
Pictionary: draw a word for your team against the clock, with no letters or numbers.
Telestrations: write a phrase, the next player draws it, the next writes what they see, and so on; reveal the chains at the end.
A Fake Artist Goes to New York: everyone adds one line to a drawing of a secret word, except the fake, who doesn't know it and must blend in.
Exquisite Corpse: fold a sheet in three and draw the head, body, and legs of a creature without seeing the other parts.
Dough Sculptures: model a word out of spare dough while your team guesses, like the clay round in Cranium.
Marshmallow Challenge: in 18 minutes, teams build the tallest free-standing tower from 20 sticks of dry spaghetti, tape, and string, with a marshmallow on top.
Blind Drawing: draw a dish blindfolded while a partner describes it; the table votes for the best.

BLUFFING AND HIDDEN ROLES
Werewolf: hidden villagers and werewolves; each night the wolves pick a victim, and each day everyone argues and votes someone out.
Wink Murder: a secret killer murders by winking, victims die dramatically, and a detective tries to catch the killer.
Spyfall: everyone secretly sees the same word except the spy; take turns asking each other questions, then vote out the spy, who wins by guessing the word.
Two Truths and a Lie: tell three things about yourself; the others guess the lie.
Liar's Dice: everyone shakes dice under a cup and bids on how many of a face are on the table; call "liar" to check.
Skull: each player plays coasters face down, one secretly marked; bid how many you can flip without hitting a skull.
Cockroach Poker: pass a card face down and say what it is; the receiver calls truth or lie, or peeks and passes it on with a new claim.
Psychiatrist: one player leaves, the rest adopt a secret rule, like answering as the person on their left, and the psychiatrist asks questions to crack it.
Paranoia: whisper a "who's most likely to" question to your neighbor, who answers aloud with a name; a coin flip decides whether the question is revealed.
Pitch It: draw two random cards and pitch the product they make to a buyer chosen by the table, who buys the best pitch. Two ingredients make a focaccia.

TABLE AND REACTION
Ninja: everyone strikes a pose; in turn, make one swift move to hit someone's hand, and they may dodge with one move.
Slapjack: flip cards one at a time; the first to slap the target card wins the pile.
Pig: pass cards around to collect four of a kind, then quietly touch your nose; the last to notice is the pig.
Buzz: count around the table, saying "buzz" instead of any multiple of seven or number with a seven in it.
Zip Zap Zop: pass a clap around the circle with the words in rhythm; hesitate or say the wrong one and you're out.
Red Hands: one player holds their palms over the other's and tries to pull away before getting slapped.
Bottle Cap Shuffleboard: flick bottle caps down the table; the closest to the edge without falling off wins.
Jenga: take a block from the tower and stack it on top without toppling it.

PHYSICAL AND RELAYS
Pass the Orange: teams pass an orange down the line from chin to chin, no hands.
Spaghetti Pick-Up: with a stick of dry spaghetti in your mouth, pick up as much penne as you can in a minute.
Face the Cookie: put a cookie on your forehead and get it into your mouth using only your face.
Stack Attack: build and then unstack a pyramid of plastic cups, fastest wins.
Egg and Spoon Race: carry an egg, or a cherry tomato, on a spoon held in your mouth.
Statues: dance until the music stops, then freeze; anyone who moves is out.
Grandma's Footsteps: creep up on a player facing the wall; if they turn and see you move, go back to the start.
Musical Chairs: one chair fewer than players; when the music stops, whoever has no seat is out.
Rock, Paper, Scissors Cheer Tournament: losers become cheerleaders for whoever beat them, so the final is a two-player roar.
Sardines: one player hides, and whoever finds them squeezes in too, until the last seeker is left.
Windowpane: stretch a ball of dough as thin as you can without tearing it; hold it to the light to judge.
Olive Pit Spitting: the farthest spit wins.

CONVERSATION
The Shoe Game: a couple or pair sits back to back, each holding one of their own shoes and one of the other's, and raises the shoe that answers "who's the better cook?"
Hot Seat: answer rapid-fire questions for a minute with no pauses and no "um".
Most Likely To: read a prompt like "most likely to eat pineapple pizza", and everyone points at once.
Never Have I Ever: hold up five fingers and put one down for each thing you've done.
Would You Rather: pick one of two dilemmas and defend your choice.
Fortunately, Unfortunately: build a story one sentence each, alternating good and bad turns.

FOOD AND KITCHEN
Kim's Game: study a tray of twenty objects for a minute; then one disappears, or the tray is covered, and you name what's missing or list what you remember.
Chopped: teams cook a snack from a mystery basket in ten minutes, and a judge picks the winner.
